# Physical infrastructure standards (hardware type approval)

From 1st July 2025, no new type of physical infrastructure or hardware may be connected to any Dorset HealthCare network without type approval from the infrastructure technical design authority (TDA). This is so we can ensure that the organisation is buying technology that has functionality, connectivity and security arrangements commensurate with its use case, and that is aligned with local and national principles, standards and best practice.

## Things we may assess
All hardware and any accompanying service (known together as a _solution_) shall receive a baseline assessment to ensure at a basic level that they can be connected to the network in a meaningful way and do not pose a security threat to the organisation.

Depending on the proposed or envisaged use case we may further evaluate:

* a [digital technology assessment criteria (DTAC)](https://transform.england.nhs.uk/key-tools-and-info/digital-technology-assessment-criteria-dtac/) submission from the manufacturer (if present and applicable);
* a [manufacturer disclosure statement (MDS2)](https://www.nema.org/standards/view/manufacturer-disclosure-statement-for-medical-device-security) (if present and applicable);
* the manufacturer's stated position against standards like [DAPB0086 (the NHS Data Security and Protection Toolkit, commonly known as DSPT)](https://www.dsptoolkit.nhs.uk), [ISO/IEC 27001 (Information Security Management Systems)](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) and [Cyber Essentials](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/cyberessentials/overview).

## What we are looking to assure
The precise assurance process will depend on the technology and the proposed use case. When considering a new solution we may look to assure ourselves around (including but not limited to):
* the overall technical compatibility of the solution with the rest of our IT environment;
* how the manufacturer approaches the lifecycle of the solution, from implementation through maintenance to final decommissioning;
* the alignment of the solution with our IT standards;
* the alignment of the solution with national principles, standards and best practice;
* any dependencies the solution has on other software and hardware;
* the accessibility of the solution;
* the cyber security posture of the solution and the manufacturer;
* the manufacturer's approach to data protection (in conjunction with the Data Protection Officer);
* the manufacturer's approach to data portability and reuse (where data is collected).

## Outcomes
We will offer the following outcomes:
* approved (substantial assurance);
* approved (limited assurance);
* approved (with conditions);
* refused.

Type approval may be requested through the usual digital change request process. Budget managers should avoid the purchase or letting of physical infrastructure or hardware that is not type approved as a connection may not be approved.
